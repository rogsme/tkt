tkt auto drives one ticket through the lifecycle and pauses where a person
decides. It reads the same facts as tkt ls, then:

  - starts the ticket when it has no worktree yet, on --as PRESET when given;
    with --approve-plan its start line says the run is unattended, so
    start-ticket skips the browser plan presentation and asks every open
    question with the harness's question tool;
  - waits while the agent works, or waits on its own background task such
    as a CI watcher;
  - approves the plan when start-ticket ends with AWAITING_APPROVAL, but only
    with --approve-plan; without it the loop pauses there for you;
  - waits for CI, then for the review bot in CI to review the new head;
  - merges a moved base itself when the merge is clean, as tkt sync does,
    with no agent and no fix round; on a conflict it runs resolve, which only
    merges and resolves (it runs git merge-tree on each look to tell the
    two apart);
  - runs address on findings, and ci when checks fail, each in a fresh
    session;
  - ends with success when the head is reviewed clean: the bot's CLEAR, or
    review-pr's READY_FOR_HUMAN. review-pr runs only in a repository with no
    review bot, [review] bot_wait = "0s".

It never merges, never runs verify, and never removes a worktree.

Where a person decides, the loop pauses instead of ending: when the agent
asks a question or ends with Status: BLOCKED (needs_you), when the plan waits
and --approve-plan is off (plan_awaits_approval), when no agent runs
(agent_missing), when the bot has not reviewed a head after [review] bot_wait
(bot_silent), after [auto] max_rounds fix runs since the plan's approval
(round_limit), when a step would repeat on the same head with nothing
changed (no_progress), and while the ticket is on hold (hold, with no
notification). It records the pause, which tkt ls shows as
auto.paused, notifies once with --notify, and goes on by itself when the
ticket moves, such as after you answer the agent in its tab or approve the
plan.

A loop ends only when the ticket is ready for you, merged or closed, or with
tkt auto --stop, which it notices at its next look. Ctrl-C ends it with exit 5
and leaves the ticket marked for auto, so tkt ls shows auto.wanted. While a
loop drives the ticket, its tab reads ACM-12 (auto).

At most [auto] max_parallel tkt auto agents work at once in a repository. A
loop holds a slot while its agent works and gives it up while it waits for CI,
the bot or you. A ticket waiting for a slot shows as queued in tkt ls, even
before it has a worktree.

--once takes at most one step and exits 0, saying what it did, what it waits
for, or why it would stop or pause. With --dry-run it prints the step instead.
--notify shows a herdr notification when the loop pauses for you or ends.
